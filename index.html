From priorities to use

A priority is not
yet a routine.

Adults who place greater importance on ingredients report more frequent use. Still, 64.9% of this group report using face sunscreen never, rarely, or sometimes.

The survey shows an association. Ingredient importance is not product quality or satisfaction, and the comparison does not establish causation.

We still need to understand how these priorities play out during outdoor activities.

Research participants

Adults who enjoy outdoor activities and have experienced sunburn.

Primary research question

How do adults who enjoy outdoor activities and have experienced sunburn decide whether and how to protect themselves from sun exposure?

Design aim to explore

Support enjoyable outdoor experiences while reducing the risk of sunburn.

Sunburn history and outdoor enjoyment were not measured in this survey. This group is a focus for primary research.

Methods and limits

Source: CDC/NCHS Rapid Surveys System, Round 1, August 2023. Total sample: 7,599 U.S. adults. Analysis weight: WEIGHT_M1.

All percentages apply the analysis weight to valid responses for the specified measure or subgroup. Missing, refused, and unknown responses are excluded from each denominator.

The use questions refer to being outdoors in the sun during warm weather. Face use excludes makeup or moisturizer containing sunscreen.

Interpretation

This is a cross-sectional self-report survey. It cannot establish individual change over time, causal effects, actual reapplication counts, shade use, sunburn occurrence, or product effectiveness.

Confidence intervals and significance tests are not shown. Differences are descriptive. Coverage and nonresponse bias may remain after weighting.

Beach characters are decorative illustrations, not respondents. The 100-icon arrays represent rounded percentages; skin color never encodes data.

Behind the visualization

A beach story, grounded in survey data.

This existing dataset connects beliefs, reported sunscreen use, and purchase considerations to a thesis about enjoyable outdoor experiences. It offers patterns to investigate through interviews, rather than explanations for individual choices.

01 / PROPORTION

100-icon arrays

Make the share of adults easy to grasp. A fresh array marks the subgroup change; exact labels accompany rounded icon counts.

02 / COMPARISON

Aligned bars

Make product priorities and subgroup differences easier to compare. Separate ratings are not parts of one whole, so a pie chart would mislead.

03 / NARRATIVE

Scroll-led sequence

An estimate-and-reveal interaction, response regrouping, and chapter transitions let readers explore the findings. A funnel was not selected because these data do not track people over time.

Interaction references: The Pudding: A Love Story and Growing Up With K-Pop. Original artwork and story retained for this project.
